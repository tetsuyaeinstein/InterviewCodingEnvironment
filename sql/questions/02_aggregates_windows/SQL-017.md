# SQL-017 · Spending quartiles for the founding cohort

**Topic:** aggregates & windows · **Level:** medium · **Interview time:** ~20 min · **Mode:** query

## Business context

The loyalty team wants to split the **founding cohort** (everyone who registered on launch day,
**2023-12-01 UTC**) into four spending tiers for 2025. Two teams need two different tier
definitions, and they keep arguing because customers with **exactly the same spend** ended up
in different tiers in last year's spreadsheet:

- **Campaign planning** needs four groups of (almost) **equal size**, and accepts that equal
  spenders may be split, as long as the split is **reproducible**.
- **Customer care** promises customers that "people who spent the same are treated the same",
  so equal spend must **always** mean the same tier, even if the groups become uneven.

Produce both tier columns side by side.

## Source tables

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `customers` | one row per customer | `customer_id`, `registered_at` (timestamptz, UTC) |
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `customer_id`, `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `gross_amount`, `transaction_status` |

## Requirements

1. Cohort: customers with `registered_at` in `[2023-12-01 00:00:00 UTC, 2023-12-02 00:00:00 UTC)`.
   Call the cohort size **N**.
2. `spend_2025`: `SUM(gross_amount)` of the customer's `COMPLETED` transactions with
   `transaction_ts` in `[2025-01-01 00:00:00 UTC, 2026-01-01 00:00:00 UTC)`. Customers without
   such transactions **are included** with `spend_2025 = 0.00`.
3. `ntile_quartile` (equal-size tiers): sort the cohort by `spend_2025` **descending**, then
   `customer_id` **ascending**. Cut that list into 4 consecutive groups whose sizes differ by at
   most one, with the larger groups first. Tier 1 = the first group (highest spenders).
4. `tie_safe_quartile` (equal spend → equal tier): for each customer let **s** be the number of
   cohort customers whose `spend_2025` is **greater than or equal to** theirs (counting
   themselves). Then `tie_safe_quartile = ceil(4 × s / N)`. Tier 1 = highest spenders. A group of
   equal spenders always gets the tier of its **last** member, so ties are pushed down, never up.
5. One row per cohort customer.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `customer_id` | integer | every cohort customer exactly once |
| `spend_2025` | numeric | 2 decimals, `0.00` for non-buyers |
| `ntile_quartile` | integer | 1 … 4 |
| `tie_safe_quartile` | integer | 1 … 4 |

Ordering: none required.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

N = 8, already sorted by spend desc, id asc:

| customer | spend | ntile_quartile | s | tie_safe_quartile = ceil(4s/8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 90 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 14 | 70 | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 12 | 70 | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| 13 | 50 | 2 | 4 | 2 |
| 15 | 20 | 3 | 5 | 3 |
| 16 | 0 | 3 | 8 | 4 |
| 17 | 0 | 4 | 8 | 4 |
| 18 | 0 | 4 | 8 | 4 |

(Customers 14 and 12 tie at 70: equal-size tiers split them by id; tie-safe tiers keep them
together in tier 2.)

## Edge cases you must handle

- Several customers with identical spend straddling a tier boundary.
- Cohort members with no transactions at all, or only non-completed ones (spend 0.00).
- Customers who registered on other days (not in the cohort, even with the same spend).
- Purchases outside 2025.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, valid PostgreSQL 17.
- Be ready to explain why the two columns differ for some customers, and which definition you
  would recommend.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Is tier 1 the top or the bottom?
- Are non-buyers part of the population (they change N and every boundary)?
- What exactly happens to a tie group that crosses a boundary?
- Which time zone defines "registered on launch day" and "2025"?
