# SQL-018 · Top three products per region with share of region revenue

**Topic:** aggregates & windows · **Level:** hard · **Interview time:** ~25 min · **Mode:** query

## In plain words

> "In each region, who are the top three products by 2025 net revenue, and what share of the region revenue does each make up?"

**You return:** one row per top product per region: `region`, `revenue_rank`, `product_id`, `product_name`, `net_revenue`, `pct_of_region_revenue`.

**The fine print, in short:**
- Only lines of **completed** 2025 (UTC) transactions, guests included. Net is quantity times price minus the line discount. Refunds are ignored.
- A product sold in several stores of a region is one row. Ties share a rank and all stay, so a region can return more than three.
- The percentage uses the revenue of all products in the region, rounded to 2 decimals.

## Business context

The merchandising director reviews 2025 per **sales region** and asks: "What were the three
best-selling products in each region by revenue, and how much of the region's total revenue did
each of them bring in?" Regions are where the **store** is, not where the customer lives. The
previous version of this report double counted revenue for multi-line baskets and quietly cut
ties, so the numbers did not reconcile with finance.

## Source tables

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `transaction_id`, `store_id`, `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `transaction_status`, `gross_amount` |
| `transaction_items` | one row per order line | `transaction_id`, `product_id`, `quantity`, `unit_price`, `discount_amount` |
| `stores` | one row per store | `store_id`, `region` |
| `products` | one row per product | `product_id`, `product_name` |

## Requirements

1. Scope: lines of `COMPLETED` transactions with `transaction_ts` in
   `[2025-01-01 00:00:00 UTC, 2026-01-01 00:00:00 UTC)`, all stores. Guest checkouts count.
2. Line net revenue = `quantity × unit_price − discount_amount` (the discount is for the whole
   line). Refunds are ignored in this report.
3. `net_revenue` of a product in a region = sum of its line net revenue over all stores of
   that region (a product sold in several stores of the region is **one** row).
4. `revenue_rank`: rank of the product within its region by `net_revenue`, highest first.
   Products with equal revenue share a rank, and the following rank skips accordingly
   (1, 2, 2, 4).
5. Return every product whose `revenue_rank ≤ 3`. All tied products are kept, so a region can
   return more than three rows.
6. `pct_of_region_revenue` = `100 × net_revenue / region total`, rounded to 2 decimals, where the
   region total is the net revenue of **all** products in that region (not only the top three).

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `region` | text | `stores.region` |
| `revenue_rank` | integer | 1 … 3 |
| `product_id` | integer | |
| `product_name` | text | |
| `net_revenue` | numeric | 2 decimals |
| `pct_of_region_revenue` | numeric | 2 decimals |

Ordering: none required.

## Edge cases you must handle

- Transactions with several lines (and a product on two lines of the same transaction).
- Line discounts.
- Non-completed transactions (including a large cancelled basket).
- A product sold in several stores of one region.
- Equal revenue at the rank-3 boundary: the tie rule above must hold even if today's data has no such tie.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, valid PostgreSQL 17.
- About 47k order lines in the small seed. The interviewer will ask about 500M lines.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Region of the store or of the customer? Current region or region at the time of sale?
- Revenue before or after discounts and refunds?
- Top three products, or top three revenue levels? What happens to ties?
- Is the percentage of the region total, or of the top-three total?
