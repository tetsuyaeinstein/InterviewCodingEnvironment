# SQL-013 · Seven-calendar-day rolling store revenue

**Topic:** aggregates & windows · **Level:** medium · **Interview time:** ~20 min · **Mode:** query

## In plain words

> "For store 4 (15 Jan to 31 Mar 2025) and store 5 (1 Nov to 7 Dec 2025), give me daily revenue and the total over the last seven days, for every day."

**You return:** one row per store per day of its period: `store_id`, `business_date`, `daily_revenue` (0.00 if no sales), `rolling_7d_revenue`.

**The fine print, in short:**
- Revenue is from **completed** transactions, by UTC date. Both the first and last dates are included.
- Days without sales still get a row.
- The seven days are the day itself and the six before it, and they may reach back before the first report date.

## Business context

Store operations reviews two small stores with a "trailing 7 days revenue" chart: for every
calendar day, the revenue of that day plus the **6 calendar days before it**. These stores do
not trade every day, and the chart must show **every** day of the review period, including days
with no sales, so that drops are visible.

## Source table

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `store_id`, `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `gross_amount`, `transaction_status` |

## Parameters (fixed)

| store_id | first report date | last report date |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2025-01-15 | 2025-03-31 |
| 5 | 2025-11-01 | 2025-12-07 |

Both bounds are **inclusive** report dates.

## Requirements

1. Revenue = `SUM(gross_amount)` of `COMPLETED` transactions. The business date is the **UTC
   calendar date** of `transaction_ts`.
2. One row per store per calendar day of its report period (76 days for store 4, 37 for
   store 5), **including days without any sale**.
3. `daily_revenue`: revenue of that store on that day; `0.00` when there were no sales.
4. `rolling_7d_revenue`: revenue of that store over the 7 calendar days `[day − 6, day]`.
   Sales on days **before** the first report date still count in the trailing window of the
   first report days.
5. Days outside the report period are not returned.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `store_id` | integer | |
| `business_date` | date | UTC calendar date |
| `daily_revenue` | numeric | never NULL; 2 decimals |
| `rolling_7d_revenue` | numeric | never NULL; 2 decimals |

Ordering: none required.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

Report period June 3 … June 10; sales on May 30 (10.00), June 4 (20.00), June 10 (5.00).

| business_date | daily_revenue | rolling_7d_revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 06-03 | 0.00 | 10.00 (window May 28 … Jun 3) |
| 06-04 | 20.00 | 30.00 |
| 06-05 | 0.00 | 30.00 (May 30 … Jun 5) |
| 06-06 | 0.00 | 20.00 (May 31 … Jun 6) |
| … | | |
| 06-10 | 5.00 | 25.00 (Jun 4 … Jun 10) |

## Edge cases you must handle

- Days without sales (inside the period and before the first sale).
- `CANCELLED` and `DECLINED` transactions on days that also have, or neighbour, real sales.
- Sales in the 6 days before the first report date.
- The trailing window counts **calendar** days, not "the last 7 days that had sales".

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, valid PostgreSQL 17.
- Be ready to explain why your window definition is correct on sparse data.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Is the window 7 calendar days or 7 trading days? Does it include the current day?
- Should days with no sales appear, and with 0 or NULL?
- Does the window look back before the report period?
- UTC or store-local days? (Store 4 is in Phoenix; this report uses UTC.)
