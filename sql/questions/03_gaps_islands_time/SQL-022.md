# SQL-022 · Every purchase streak with start, end and length

**Topic:** gaps, islands & time · **Level:** medium · **Interview time:** ~20 min · **Mode:** query

## Business context

The CRM team wants a "streak history" table to drive habit nudges ("you are on day 3, come back
tomorrow") and to analyse how streaks start and break. Unlike a yes/no flag, they need **every**
streak with its exact boundaries, so it can be joined to the campaigns that ran on those dates.
The production fact holds about 500 million transactions.

## Source table

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `customer_id`, `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `transaction_status` |

## Definitions

- A **purchase day** of a customer is a UTC calendar date on which that customer has at least
  one `COMPLETED` transaction. Several purchases on one day are one purchase day.
- A **streak** is a *maximal* run of consecutive calendar purchase days: the day before its first
  day and the day after its last day are not purchase days.

## Requirements

1. Only `transaction_status = 'COMPLETED'` counts. Guests (`customer_id IS NULL`) are excluded.
2. Calendar day = UTC date of `transaction_ts`.
3. Return **every** streak that is **at least 3 days long**, over the whole data history (no date
   filter). A customer can have several rows.
4. Streaks are maximal and never overlap: a 7-day run is one row of length 7, not several
   3-day windows.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `customer_id` | integer | |
| `streak_start` | date | first purchase day of the streak (inclusive) |
| `streak_end` | date | last purchase day of the streak (inclusive) |
| `streak_days` | integer | number of days in the streak = `streak_end - streak_start + 1` |

One row per streak. Ordering: none required (compared as an unordered set).

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

| customer_id | completed purchase dates (UTC) |
|---|---|
| 701 | Oct 1, Oct 2, Oct 2, Oct 3, Oct 9, Oct 10, Oct 11, Oct 12 |
| 702 | Feb 27, Feb 28, Mar 1 (non-leap year), Mar 5 |
| 703 | Jun 1, Jun 2, Jun 4, Jun 5 |

Expected output:

| customer_id | streak_start | streak_end | streak_days |
|---|---|---|---|
| 701 | Oct 1 | Oct 3 | 3 |
| 701 | Oct 9 | Oct 12 | 4 |
| 702 | Feb 27 | Mar 1 | 3 |

(703 has only two 2-day runs.)

## Edge cases you must handle

- Several purchases on the same day must neither lengthen a streak nor split it.
- A customer with two separate streaks → two rows.
- Streaks crossing a month end, a year end and the leap day 2024-02-29.
- A day whose only transactions are `CANCELLED` / `PENDING` is a gap.
- A run of exactly 3 days is included; a run of 2 days is not.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, PostgreSQL 17.
- Use PostgreSQL date arithmetic (`date - integer`); no T-SQL `DATEADD`/`DATEDIFF`.
- Be ready to discuss the cost on 500M rows and how to maintain this table incrementally.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Is the streak defined on UTC days or store-local days?
- Should a streak that is still "open" at the end of the data be reported, and how is it flagged?
- Do later refunds remove a purchase day?
- Which minimum length is interesting for the business?
