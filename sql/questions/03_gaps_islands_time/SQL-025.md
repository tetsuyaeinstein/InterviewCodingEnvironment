# SQL-025 · Day-7 and first-week retention of the February cohort

**Topic:** gaps, islands & time · **Level:** easy · **Interview time:** ~15 min · **Mode:** query

## Business context

The growth team tracks how many new customers come back shortly after their first purchase.
Two definitions are in use and keep getting mixed up in dashboards, so this report computes
**both**, side by side, for the customers acquired in **February 2025**.

## Source table

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `customer_id`, `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `transaction_status` |

## Definitions

- A purchase is a `COMPLETED` transaction. All dates are **UTC calendar dates** of `transaction_ts`.
- **First purchase date** (day 0) = the UTC date of the customer's earliest completed purchase
  over **all** history.
- **Cohort** = customers whose first purchase date is in February 2025
  (2025-02-01 … 2025-02-28 inclusive).
- **Day-7 retained** = the customer has a completed purchase on the calendar date
  `first_purchase_date + 7` exactly.
- **Week-1 retained** = the customer has a completed purchase on any calendar date from
  `first_purchase_date + 1` to `first_purchase_date + 7` (both inclusive). Further purchases on
  day 0 itself do not count.
- Both are calendar-date definitions. The time of day, and the number of hours elapsed since
  the first purchase, play no role.

## Requirements

1. Every cohort customer appears exactly once, retained or not.
2. Guests (`customer_id IS NULL`) are excluded.
3. Both flags are `true` or `false`, never NULL.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `customer_id` | integer | one row per cohort customer |
| `first_purchase_date` | date | day 0 |
| `retained_day_7` | boolean | purchase exactly on day 0 + 7 |
| `retained_week_1` | boolean | purchase on any of days 1 … 7 |

Ordering: none required.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

| customer_id | completed purchases (UTC) | day 7 | week 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1501 | Feb 10 09:00, Feb 17 18:00 | true | true |
| 1502 | Feb 10, Feb 12, Feb 18 | false | true (Feb 12 is day 2; Feb 18 is day 8) |
| 1503 | Feb 10 08:00, Feb 10 19:00 | false | false |
| 1504 | Jan 20, Feb 11 | not in the cohort (first purchase in January) | |

## Edge cases you must handle

- A purchase late on day 0 and early on day 7 is day-7 retention, even though fewer than
  7 × 24 hours have passed.
- A customer whose only purchase in the week is on day 8 is not retained on either flag.
- A non-completed transaction before February does not make February's purchase a repeat.
- Cohort customers with no further purchase at all must still be listed.
- Day 0 near the end of February: day 7 falls in March.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, PostgreSQL 17.
- PostgreSQL date arithmetic (`date + integer`); no `DATEADD`.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Exact day 7, any day within the first week, or "within 168 hours"? Calendar dates or hours?
- Which time zone defines the day?
- Is the cohort defined by first purchase or by registration date?
