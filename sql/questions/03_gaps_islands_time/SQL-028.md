# SQL-028 · Point-in-time list price for each sales line

**Topic:** gaps, islands & time · **Level:** medium · **Interview time:** ~20 min · **Mode:** query

## In plain words

> "For sales lines in the first half of 2025, what was the list price at the moment of sale, and how far was the charged price from it?"

**You return:** one row per line: `transaction_id`, `line_number`, `product_id`, `transaction_ts`, `unit_price`, `list_price`, `price_diff`.

**The fine print, in short:**
- Only lines of **completed** transactions from 1 Jan to 30 Jun 2025 (UTC), for products 1, 3, 7 and 9.
- Use the price valid at that moment; at the exact instant of a change, the new price applies. Do not use the current price.
- Products with no price history stay in, with `list_price` and `price_diff` NULL.

## Business context

Loss-prevention wants to audit manual price overrides at the till. For each sales line they need
the **list price that was in force at the moment of the sale**, next to the price actually
charged. Comparing with today's price is useless: prices change, sometimes in the middle of a day.

## Source tables

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `transaction_id`, `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `transaction_status` |
| `transaction_items` | one row per order line | `transaction_id`, `line_number`, `product_id`, `unit_price` numeric(10,2) |
| `product_price_history` | one row per price version | `product_id`, `valid_from`, `valid_to` (timestamptz), `price` numeric(10,2) |

A price version is valid on the half-open interval `[valid_from, valid_to)`; `valid_to IS NULL`
means it is still valid. Versions of one product never overlap. `products.standard_price` is
the **current** price and must not be used.

## Requirements

1. Scope: lines of `COMPLETED` transactions with `transaction_ts` in
   **[2025-01-01 00:00, 2025-07-01 00:00) UTC**, for **products 1, 3, 7 and 9**.
2. `list_price` = the `price` of the version whose interval contains `transaction_ts`. At the
   exact instant of a price change, the **new** price applies.
3. Some products (for example open-price items whose price is typed in at the till) have no
   price history at all. Their lines **must still be returned**, with `list_price` and
   `price_diff` NULL.
4. `price_diff` = `unit_price - list_price` (negative = charged below list).
5. Exactly one output row per input line. A product may appear on two lines of one transaction;
   both lines are returned.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `transaction_id` | bigint | |
| `line_number` | integer | |
| `product_id` | integer | |
| `transaction_ts` | timestamptz | |
| `unit_price` | numeric | price charged |
| `list_price` | numeric | effective list price at `transaction_ts`, NULL if none |
| `price_diff` | numeric | `unit_price - list_price`, NULL if no list price |

Ordering: none required.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

Price history of product 501: `[2024-01-01, 2025-02-10 09:00) = 3.00`,
`[2025-02-10 09:00, NULL) = 3.40`.

| line sold at | unit_price | list_price | price_diff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-02-10 08:59:59 | 3.00 | 3.00 | 0.00 |
| 2025-02-10 09:00:00 | 3.40 | 3.40 | 0.00 |
| 2025-02-11 12:00:00 | 3.00 | 3.40 | -0.40 |

## Edge cases you must handle

- A sale one second before and exactly at a price change.
- Changes at midnight and in the middle of the day.
- The current version has `valid_to IS NULL`.
- Products without any price history.
- Manual overrides where `unit_price` differs from the list price.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, PostgreSQL 17.
- The join must not create duplicate lines.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Is `valid_to` inclusive or exclusive? Which price wins at the change instant?
- What should happen to lines with no matching version: drop, NULL, or fall back to the current price?
- Are price changes effective in UTC or in the store's local time?
