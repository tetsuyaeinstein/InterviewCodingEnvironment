# SQL-030 · Gaps in daily device reports

**Topic:** gaps, islands & time · **Level:** hard · **Interview time:** ~30 min · **Mode:** query

## Business context

Every kiosk and POS terminal is supposed to send one **scheduled** health report per UTC day
(around 02:00). When reports stop, the device is either broken or offline, and the operations
team wants a list of **reporting gaps** for January and February 2025: each run of consecutive
days without a scheduled report, per device, so they can open one ticket per gap rather than
one per day.

## Source tables

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `devices` | one row per device | `device_id` (text), `installed_on` (date), `decommissioned_on` (date, NULL = still in service) |
| `device_status_events` | one row per status message | `device_id`, `event_ts` (timestamptz, UTC, when the device produced it), `received_at` (timestamptz, when we got it), `event_kind` (`SCHEDULED` / `CHANGE`) |

## Definitions

- Period: UTC dates **2025-01-01 … 2025-02-28** (inclusive).
- A device is **in service** on a date `d` if `installed_on <= d` and
  (`decommissioned_on IS NULL` or `d < decommissioned_on`): the decommission date is the first
  day out of service.
- A date `d` is **reported** for a device if at least one `SCHEDULED` event of that device has
  `event_ts` on UTC date `d`. `CHANGE` events do not count. A report that was **received late**
  still counts for the day of its `event_ts`. Several reports on one day count once.
- A date is **missing** if the device is in service that day and the date is not reported.
- A **reporting gap** is a maximal run of consecutive missing dates of one device within the
  period.

## Requirements

1. Return one row per reporting gap, for every device (all device types).
2. Days on which the device is not in service are never missing, and they are not part of any
   gap. Days outside the period are ignored (a gap that would continue into March ends on 02-28).
3. Devices without gaps do not appear.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `device_id` | text | |
| `gap_start` | date | first missing date of the gap |
| `gap_end` | date | last missing date of the gap (inclusive) |
| `missing_days` | integer | `gap_end - gap_start + 1` |

Ordering: none required.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

Device `POS-X7`, installed 2025-01-03, decommissioned 2025-01-12. Scheduled reports on
01-03, 01-04, 01-05 (two), 01-08 (received on 01-10), 01-11. A `CHANGE` event on 01-07.

| device_id | gap_start | gap_end | missing_days |
|---|---|---|---|
| POS-X7 | 2025-01-06 | 2025-01-07 | 2 |
| POS-X7 | 2025-01-09 | 2025-01-10 | 2 |

01-01 and 01-02 are before installation; from 01-12 on the device is decommissioned.

## Edge cases you must handle

- A device that goes silent for weeks while still in service.
- A device decommissioned during the period (no gaps after its last service day).
- A device installed during the period.
- A report received a day late; two reports on one day.
- A day that only has `CHANGE` events.
- A gap that starts on the first day of the period.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, PostgreSQL 17.
- Your answer must find days that have **no rows at all**; think about where those days come from.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Which timestamp defines the report day: produced (`event_ts`) or received?
- Does a `CHANGE` event prove the device was alive that day?
- Is the decommission date itself a service day?
- Would a "gap longer than 36 hours between reports" definition give a different answer?
