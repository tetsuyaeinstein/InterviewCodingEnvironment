# SQL-027 · Merge overlapping downtime incidents and total the minutes

**Topic:** gaps, islands & time · **Level:** hard · **Interview time:** ~30 min · **Mode:** query

## Business context

The kiosk vendor's SLA credits are based on **minutes of downtime per device per month**. Outages
are reported independently by automated monitoring, by field technicians and by the vendor
itself, so one real outage often appears as several incident rows that overlap, contain each
other, follow each other back-to-back, or are exact duplicates. Finance needs the January 2025
figures **without double counting**, together with the number of distinct outages.

## Source table

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `device_downtime_incidents` | one row per reported incident | `incident_id`, `device_id` (text), `down_from`, `down_to` (timestamptz, UTC) |

Each incident is the half-open interval `[down_from, down_to)`, with `down_to > down_from`.
`reported_by` is irrelevant here.

## Requirements

1. Reporting window: **[2025-01-01 00:00, 2025-02-01 00:00) UTC**. Only the part of an
   incident that lies inside the window counts: an incident that starts in December or ends in
   February contributes only its January minutes.
2. Per device, combine incidents into **outages**: two incidents belong to the same outage if
   their intervals overlap **or touch** (one ends exactly when the other starts). This applies
   transitively: A overlaps B and B touches C → one outage A ∪ B ∪ C.
3. Duplicated and nested incidents add nothing beyond the outage that contains them.
4. `downtime_minutes` = total length of the device's outages inside the window, in minutes,
   **rounded to 2 decimals** (seconds count: 90 seconds = 1.50 minutes).
5. `outage_count` = number of outages (after combining) that intersect the window.
6. Only devices with at least one incident intersecting the window appear.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `device_id` | text | one row per device |
| `outage_count` | integer | combined outages in January 2025 |
| `downtime_minutes` | numeric | total January downtime, no double counting, 2 decimals |

Ordering: none required.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

Device `KIOSK-X9`, incidents on 2025-01-07:

| incident | interval | note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [06:00, 07:00) | |
| 2 | [06:30, 06:40) | inside 1 |
| 3 | [06:50, 07:20) | overlaps 1 |
| 4 | [07:20, 07:30) | touches 3 |
| 5 | [09:00, 09:15) | separate |
| 6 | [09:00, 09:15) | duplicate of 5 |

Outages: [06:00, 07:30) = 90 minutes and [09:00, 09:15) = 15 minutes →
`KIOSK-X9 | 2 | 105.00`. (Summing the raw rows would give 140 minutes.)

## Edge cases you must handle

- Overlapping, nested, touching and exactly duplicated incidents.
- Outages crossing midnight.
- An incident that starts in January but ends in February (clip it).
- A long incident containing a short one, followed by a third incident that starts after the
  short one has ended but while the long one is still open (all three are one outage).

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, PostgreSQL 17.
- Be ready to explain why a pairwise self-join over overlapping incidents is not a correct (or
  scalable) way to remove the double counting.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Do touching intervals merge? Is a 1-second gap between two incidents a separate outage?
- Is downtime clipped to the month or attributed to the month in which the outage started?
- Should sources be trusted differently (e.g. ignore VENDOR reports when MONITORING disagrees)?
