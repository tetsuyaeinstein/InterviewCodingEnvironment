# SQL-040 · Monthly net sales with partial refunds and units, without fan-out

**Topic:** dimensional modeling · **Level:** hard · **Interview time:** ~30 min · **Mode:** query

## In plain words

> "Give me monthly net sales for October 2025 to January 2026: what we sold, the units, what we refunded, and the net."

**You return:** exactly 4 rows, one per month: `month_start`, `orders`, `units_sold`, `gross_sales`, `refunded_amount`, `net_sales`.

**The fine print, in short:**
- Sales are **completed** transactions, counted in the month of the sale (UTC). Refunds are counted in the month they are paid, whatever month the sale was in.
- Orders with several lines or several refunds must not inflate units or refunds.
- Net is gross sales minus refunds and can be negative. Months with nothing show 0, not NULL.

## Business context

Finance closes the books monthly and wants one line per month for the fourth quarter and the
month after: how much was sold, how many units, how much was refunded, and the net. The
company recognises **sales in the month of the sale** and **refunds in the month the refund is
paid**, even when the refund belongs to an earlier month's sale. The first version of this report
joined orders to lines to refunds in one go and overstated both units and refunds for orders with
several lines or several partial refunds.

## Source tables

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `transaction_id`, `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `gross_amount` numeric(12,2), `transaction_status` |
| `transaction_items` | one row per order line | `transaction_id`, `quantity` |
| `refunds` | one row per refund payment | `refund_id`, `transaction_id`, `refund_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `refund_amount` |

`transactions.gross_amount` is the exact sum of its lines. A transaction can have several lines and
several partial refunds (all refunds of a transaction total at most its `gross_amount`).

## Requirements

1. Report the four UTC calendar months **2025-10, 2025-11, 2025-12 and 2026-01**, one row each,
   even if a month has no sales.
2. Sales are `COMPLETED` transactions recognised in the month of `transaction_ts`:
   `orders` = number of such transactions, `units_sold` = sum of `quantity` over their lines,
   `gross_sales` = sum of `gross_amount`.
3. `refunded_amount` = sum of `refund_amount` of refunds whose `refund_ts` falls in the month,
   regardless of when the original sale happened (a refund in November for an October sale reduces
   November, not October).
4. `net_sales` = `gross_sales - refunded_amount`; it can be negative.
5. Months without sales or refunds show 0, not NULL. Amounts are compared rounded to 2 decimals.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `month_start` | date | first day of the UTC month |
| `orders` | integer | completed transactions |
| `units_sold` | integer | |
| `gross_sales` | numeric | |
| `refunded_amount` | numeric | positive number: money paid back in the month |
| `net_sales` | numeric | `gross_sales - refunded_amount` |

Ordering: none required.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

Order A (Sep 28, 2 lines of 3 and 2 units, 50.00) is refunded 10.00 on Oct 2 and 5.00 on Oct 3.
Order B (Oct 5, 1 line of 4 units, 20.00) is refunded 20.00 on Oct 6.

| month_start | orders | units_sold | gross_sales | refunded_amount | net_sales |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-09-01 | 1 | 5 | 50.00 | 0.00 | 50.00 |
| 2025-10-01 | 1 | 4 | 20.00 | 35.00 | -15.00 |

## Edge cases you must handle

- A transaction with several lines (units must not be multiplied by refunds, refunds must not be
  multiplied by lines).
- A transaction with several partial refunds, and one fully refunded transaction.
- A refund paid in a later month than the sale.
- The last month has refunds but no sales.
- Non-completed transactions must not contribute sales.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, PostgreSQL 17.
- In production the line table has 1.5 billion rows. Explain where you aggregate.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Are refunds recognised when paid or restated into the month of the sale?
- Is the month UTC or head-office time?
- Is the refund a cost line (negative revenue) or a separate metric?
- Should non-completed transactions with refunds exist at all (data defect)?
