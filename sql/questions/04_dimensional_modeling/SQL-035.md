# SQL-035 · Weekly active customers by ISO week in New York time

**Topic:** dimensional modeling · **Level:** easy · **Interview time:** ~15 min · **Mode:** query

## Business context

The company reports weekly KPIs on **ISO weeks** (Monday to Sunday) in **head-office time,
America/New_York**, regardless of where the sale happened. The growth team wants "weekly active
customers" for fiscal 2025, which is defined as ISO weeks **2025-W01 to 2025-W52**. Last year's
report had two bugs: the week around New Year was labelled "2024-W01", and Sunday-evening sales
landed in the following week because the week was computed on UTC timestamps.

## Source table

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `customer_id` (NULL = guest), `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `transaction_status` |

## Requirements

1. Only `COMPLETED` transactions of known customers (`customer_id IS NOT NULL`) count.
2. Convert each `transaction_ts` to **America/New_York local time** (respect daylight saving
   time), then assign it to the ISO week of that local date. ISO weeks start on Monday, and week 1
   is the week containing the first Thursday of the year. A week belongs to its **ISO year**, which
   can differ from the calendar year of some of its days.
3. Scope: ISO weeks 2025-W01 … 2025-W52 (52 rows). Purchases whose local date falls into an
   ISO week of another ISO year are excluded.
4. `active_customers` = number of **distinct** customers with at least one purchase in the week.
   `purchases` = number of qualifying transactions in the week.
5. `iso_week` label format: `IIII-Www` with the ISO year and a two-digit week, e.g. `2025-W07`.

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `iso_week` | text | e.g. `2025-W07` |
| `week_start` | date | the Monday of that ISO week |
| `active_customers` | integer | distinct customers |
| `purchases` | integer | completed transactions |

Ordering: none required.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

| transaction_ts (UTC) | New York local | ISO week |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-01-01 02:00 | 2025-12-31 21:00 (Wed) | 2026-W01 |
| 2027-01-02 12:00 | 2027-01-02 07:00 (Sat) | **2026-W53** |
| 2026-03-09 03:00 | 2026-03-08 23:00 EDT (Sun) | 2026-W10 |

## Edge cases you must handle

- Days at the turn of the year that belong to the next or previous ISO year.
- Sales late on Sunday evening in New York, which are already Monday in UTC.
- Both daylight-saving switches (March and November): the UTC offset changes during the year.
- Several purchases by the same customer in one week; guest checkouts.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, PostgreSQL 17. Use the IANA zone name, not a fixed
  offset.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Which time zone defines the week: UTC, store-local or head-office?
- ISO weeks or "weeks starting Sunday"? How are weeks spanning New Year labelled?
- Are guests "active customers"?
- Do refunds or cancellations remove a customer from the week?
