# SQL-002 · Daily completed sales per store for a fixed date range

**Topic:** foundations · **Level:** easy · **Interview time:** ~10 min · **Mode:** query

## In plain words

> "For 1 to 16 March 2025, how much did each store sell on each day? Only list store-days that actually had sales."

**You return:** one row per store and day with sales: `store_id`, `business_date`, `completed_txn_count`, `gross_amount`.

**The fine print, in short:**
- Only **completed** transactions count, and guest checkouts are included.
- The day is the UTC date, and both 1 March and 16 March are included.
- Store-days with no completed sales are not returned.

## Business context

Finance wants a store-by-day sales extract for the first half of March 2025 to reconcile against
the bank settlement files. The extract only lists store-days that actually had completed sales;
filling in empty days is done downstream.

## Source table

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `store_id`, `transaction_ts` (timestamptz, UTC), `gross_amount`, `transaction_status`, `customer_id` (NULL = guest) |

## Requirements

1. Only `transaction_status = 'COMPLETED'` transactions count.
2. The business date is the **UTC calendar date** of `transaction_ts` (not the store's local date).
3. The reporting range is the business dates **2025-03-01 through 2025-03-16, both inclusive**.
   As a timestamp interval that is the half-open range
   `[2025-03-01 00:00:00 UTC, 2025-03-17 00:00:00 UTC)`.
4. Guest checkouts **are** store sales and must be included.
5. One row per `(store_id, business_date)` that has at least one completed transaction. Store-days
   without completed sales are **not** returned (no zero rows).

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `store_id` | integer | |
| `business_date` | date | UTC calendar date |
| `completed_txn_count` | integer | number of completed transactions that store-day, always ≥ 1 |
| `gross_amount` | numeric | sum of `gross_amount` of those transactions |

Ordering: none required (unordered set). Amounts are compared at 2 decimals.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

| transaction_ts (UTC) | store | status | amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-03-01 00:00:00 | 9 | COMPLETED | 10.00 |
| 2025-03-01 22:15:00 | 9 | COMPLETED | 5.00 |
| 2025-03-02 09:00:00 | 9 | DECLINED | 40.00 |
| 2025-03-16 23:59:00 | 9 | COMPLETED | 7.00 |
| 2025-03-17 00:00:00 | 9 | COMPLETED | 3.00 |

Expected rows: `(9, 2025-03-01, 2, 15.00)` and `(9, 2025-03-16, 1, 7.00)`. March 2 has no
completed sale, and March 17 is outside the range.

## Edge cases you must handle

- A transaction at exactly midnight UTC on the first day (in) and on the day after the last day (out).
- Transactions late in the evening of the **last** day of the range.
- A store-day whose only transactions are declined or cancelled.
- Guest checkouts.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, valid PostgreSQL 17.
- In production there is an index on `(store_id, transaction_ts)` and the table is 500M rows: your
  date filter should be able to use an index on the raw timestamp column.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- UTC date or store-local date?
- Is the end date inclusive?
- Do guest sales count towards store totals?
- Should store-days with zero sales appear?
