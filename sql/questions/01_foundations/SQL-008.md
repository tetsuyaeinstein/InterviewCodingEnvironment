# SQL-008 · Top revenue categories at one store with ties

**Topic:** foundations · **Level:** medium · **Interview time:** ~15 min · **Mode:** query

## In plain words

> "At store 5 in November 2025, which product categories earned the most? Give me the top two revenue levels, and keep everyone who ties."

**You return:** one row per category in the top two levels: `category_name`, `gross_revenue`, `discount_amount`, `net_revenue`, `revenue_dense_rank`, `revenue_rank`. Sorted by net revenue (highest first), then category name; the order is graded.

**The fine print, in short:**
- Only lines of **completed** transactions at store 5, UTC dates in November 2025.
- Gross is quantity times unit price. The discount is a total for the whole line, not per unit. Net is gross minus discount.
- Tied categories all stay. One rank has no gaps after a tie, the other skips.

## Business context

The category manager of store **5** is planning shelf space for December and asks: "Which
product categories earned the most at my store in November 2025?" They want the **top two
revenue levels**: if several categories earn exactly the same amount they all deserve the shelf
space, so ties must never be cut arbitrarily. Because a promotion ran in November, they want to
see gross revenue, discounts and net revenue side by side.

## Source tables

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction | `transaction_id`, `store_id`, `transaction_ts` (UTC), `transaction_status` |
| `transaction_items` | one row per order line | `transaction_id`, `product_id`, `quantity`, `unit_price`, `discount_amount` |
| `products` | one row per product | `product_id`, `category_id` |
| `product_categories` | one row per category | `category_id`, `category_name` |

## Requirements

1. Scope: lines of `COMPLETED` transactions at `store_id = 5` with a UTC business date in
   November 2025, i.e. `transaction_ts` in `[2025-11-01 00:00:00 UTC, 2025-12-01 00:00:00 UTC)`.
2. Per category:
   - `gross_revenue = SUM(quantity × unit_price)`
   - `discount_amount = SUM(discount_amount)` (the line discount is an amount for the whole line, not per unit)
   - `net_revenue = gross_revenue − discount_amount`
3. Rank categories by `net_revenue`, highest first, and report two ranks:
   - `revenue_dense_rank`: tied categories share a rank and the next level gets the next integer (1, 1, 2, …).
   - `revenue_rank`: tied categories share a rank and the next level skips the tied positions (1, 1, 3, …).
4. Return every category whose `revenue_dense_rank` is **1 or 2** (all tied categories included).
5. Output order: `net_revenue` descending, then `category_name` ascending. **The order is graded.**

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `category_name` | text | |
| `gross_revenue` | numeric | |
| `discount_amount` | numeric | |
| `net_revenue` | numeric | |
| `revenue_dense_rank` | integer | 1 or 2 |
| `revenue_rank` | integer | |

Amounts are compared at 2 decimals.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

| category | net revenue | revenue_dense_rank | revenue_rank | returned? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Garden | 120.00 | 1 | 1 | yes |
| Toys | 95.00 | 2 | 2 | yes |
| Books | 95.00 | 2 | 2 | yes |
| Music | 80.00 | 3 | 4 | no |

## Edge cases you must handle

- Two or more categories with exactly the same net revenue, at the first and at a later level.
- A large order that was cancelled.
- Line discounts (gross and net differ).
- The same product on two lines of one transaction.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, valid PostgreSQL 17.
- Be ready to say how the answer changes if "top 2" means "top 2 categories" instead of "top 2
  revenue levels".

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- UTC month or store-local month?
- Is revenue before or after discounts? Is the discount per unit or per line?
- How should ties be handled: keep all, break by name, or break by something else?
- Do refunds reduce category revenue? (Not in this exercise.)
