# SQL-005 · Q4 net spend after refunds without join fan-out

**Topic:** foundations · **Level:** medium · **Interview time:** ~20 min · **Mode:** query

## Business context

The finance business partner for Q4 2025 wants, per customer, what they bought in the quarter
and how much of it they kept: gross spend, units sold, refunds and net spend. Refunds keep
arriving after the quarter closes; finance wants **all** refunds of Q4 sales, whenever they were
issued.

## Source tables

| table | grain | columns you need |
|---|---|---|
| `transactions` | one row per checkout transaction (header) | `transaction_id`, `customer_id` (NULL = guest), `transaction_ts` (UTC), `gross_amount`, `transaction_status` |
| `transaction_items` | one row per order line | `transaction_id`, `line_number`, `quantity` |
| `refunds` | one row per refund event; a transaction can have **several** partial refunds | `transaction_id`, `refund_ts`, `refund_amount` |

`transactions.gross_amount` always equals the sum of its lines' net values, so you may use either.

## Requirements

1. In scope: `COMPLETED` transactions of known customers whose **UTC business date** is in Q4 2025,
   i.e. `transaction_ts` in `[2025-10-01 00:00:00 UTC, 2026-01-01 00:00:00 UTC)`.
2. `refunded_amount` is the sum of **all** refunds of those transactions, **regardless of
   `refund_ts`** (a November or January refund of an October sale counts).
3. Refunds of transactions outside the scope (for example of a September sale) do not count.
4. `items_sold` is the total `quantity` over all lines of the in-scope transactions.
5. `net_spend = gross_spend − refunded_amount`.
6. One row per customer with at least one in-scope transaction. Customers without one are not returned.
7. A customer with no refunds has `refunded_amount = 0.00` (not NULL).

## Output contract

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `customer_id` | integer | |
| `completed_txn_count` | integer | number of in-scope transactions |
| `items_sold` | integer | sum of line quantities |
| `gross_spend` | numeric | sum of `gross_amount` |
| `refunded_amount` | numeric | 0.00 if none |
| `net_spend` | numeric | can be 0.00 for a fully refunded customer |

Ordering: none required (unordered set). Amounts are compared at 2 decimals.

## Illustrative example (not the real data)

Customer 711 has one Q4 transaction of 90.00 with three lines (quantities 1, 1, 2) and two
refunds of 10.00 and 5.00 (one issued in January 2026).

| customer_id | completed_txn_count | items_sold | gross_spend | refunded_amount | net_spend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 711 | 1 | 4 | 90.00 | 15.00 | 75.00 |

Note that 3 lines × 2 refunds = 6 combinations. Results such as `items_sold = 8`,
`gross_spend = 180.00` or `refunded_amount = 45.00` mean rows were multiplied somewhere.

## Edge cases you must handle

- A transaction with several lines **and** several refunds.
- A fully refunded transaction (net zero).
- Refunds issued in a later month or year than the sale.
- Customers with Q4 sales and no refunds at all.

## Constraints

- One read-only `SELECT` / `WITH` statement, valid PostgreSQL 17.
- Production: 500M transactions, 1.2B lines, 20M refunds. Explain where you aggregate and why.

## Clarify before you code (what a strong candidate asks)

- Are refunds attributed to the sale period or to the refund period?
- Can a transaction have more than one refund? Can refunds exceed the sale amount?
- Is "items sold" units (quantity) or number of lines?
- Should a customer whose net spend is zero still appear? (Yes.)
