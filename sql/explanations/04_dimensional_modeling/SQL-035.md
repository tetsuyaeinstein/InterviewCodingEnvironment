# SQL-035 · Weekly active customers by ISO week in New York time: explanation

## Core idea

Three things must happen in the right order:

1. **Instant → local wall-clock time** with the IANA zone: `transaction_ts AT TIME ZONE
   'America/New_York'`. For a `timestamptz` this returns a `timestamp` (no zone) showing New York
   wall-clock time, with the DST offset that applied **at that instant**.
2. **Local date → ISO week**, labelled with the **ISO year** (`IYYY`), not the calendar year.
3. **Count distinct customers** per week (several purchases per week are one active customer).

## Solution (`SQL-035.sql`)

`to_char(ny_date, 'IYYY-"W"IW')` gives the label. `date_trunc('week', ny_date)` gives the Monday.
The scope filter is on the local date: Monday 2024-12-30 (start of 2025-W01) up to, but not
including, Monday 2025-12-29 (start of 2026-W01).

| UTC instant | New York | ISO week (NY) | ISO week if computed on UTC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-12-30 15:00 (customer 9) | Mon 12-30 10:00 EST | **2025-W01** | 2025-W01 (but `YYYY-IW` → "2024-W01") |
| 2025-03-10 03:30 (customer 14) | **Sun 03-09 23:30 EDT** | 2025-W10 | 2025-W11 |
| 2025-11-02 05:30 / 06:30 | Sun 11-02 01:30 EDT / 01:30 EST | 2025-W44 | 2025-W44 |
| 2025-11-03 04:30 (customer 14) | **Sun 11-02 23:30 EST** | 2025-W44 | 2025-W45 |

Customer 14's two 01:30 sales on 2025-11-02 show the "repeated hour" of the autumn switch:
the local wall-clock times are equal, but the instants are an hour apart. Never deduplicate or
order on local wall-clock time.

## Alternative (`SQL-035_alt.sql`)

The same result built with `EXTRACT(isoyear ...)` and `EXTRACT(week ...)`. The more important
difference is the filter: the window bounds are **New York midnights converted to instants**
(`TIMESTAMP '2024-12-30 00:00' AT TIME ZONE 'America/New_York'`), so the predicate is on the raw
`transaction_ts` column and can use the `transactions(transaction_ts)` index and partition
pruning. Filtering on `(transaction_ts AT TIME ZONE ...)::date` cannot, unless you build an
expression index.

## Edge cases and the classic bugs

| bug | effect | fixture |
|---|---|---|
| `YYYY-IW` instead of `IYYY-IW` | 2024-12-30/31 labelled "2024-W01"; a 53rd label appears | customer 9 |
| week computed on UTC | Sunday-evening NY sales move to the next week | customer 14 (W10/W11, W44/W45) |
| fixed offset `-05:00` | wrong for the EDT half of the year. In PostgreSQL the POSIX-style `'-05:00'` even means UTC**+5** | all summer weeks |
| `COUNT(customer_id)` | counts purchases, not customers | many repeat buyers |

## Complexity and indexes

- One scan of the year's completed transactions plus a hash aggregate. `COUNT(DISTINCT)` per
  group sorts internally. Fine at this size.
- At 500M rows, `COUNT(DISTINCT)` per week is the expensive part. Options: pre-aggregate to a
  **customer-week** table (one row per customer per ISO week) maintained incrementally, then
  `COUNT(*)`. Or, if approximate is acceptable, HyperLogLog sketches (the `hll` extension; in
  Synapse/SQL Server `APPROX_COUNT_DISTINCT`). Sketches can be merged across weeks for monthly
  actives, but plain distinct counts cannot be added up.

## Production considerations

- This is the textbook case for a **date dimension**: one row per calendar date with
  `iso_year`, `iso_week`, `iso_week_label`, `week_start`, fiscal attributes and holidays. Facts
  carry a `date_key` derived from the **reporting time zone**, and reports join on it. Nobody
  rewrites ISO logic in every query.
- Store facts in UTC; derive local dates per reporting zone at load time (or in a view). If
  the business has several reporting zones, the fact needs one date key per zone, or a
  time-of-day dimension plus zone conversion.
- Late data: a purchase arriving three days late changes last week's active count. Recompute the
  last N weeks on each run.

## SQL Server / Azure Synapse notes

- Time zones: `transaction_ts AT TIME ZONE 'UTC' AT TIME ZONE 'Eastern Standard Time'`. SQL
  Server uses **Windows zone names** ('Eastern Standard Time' covers EST and EDT), not IANA names.
- ISO week: `DATEPART(iso_week, d)`. There is no ISO-year function. A well-known derivation is
  `YEAR(DATEADD(day, 26 - DATEPART(iso_week, d), d))` (it moves into the middle of the ISO year).
  Or, better, use the date dimension.
- `DATEPART(week, ...)` is **not** ISO: it depends on `@@DATEFIRST` and restarts on January 1.
- Synapse: `APPROX_COUNT_DISTINCT` is available for large-scale weekly actives.
