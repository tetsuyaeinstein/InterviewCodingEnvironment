# SQL-025 · Day-7 and first-week retention: explanation

## Approach

1. `purchase_days`: distinct `(customer_id, UTC date)` of COMPLETED, non-guest transactions.
2. `first_purchase`: `MIN(purchase_date)` per customer over **all history**. Then filter the
   cohort to February 2025. Filtering transactions to February first would put customers who had
   bought in January into the cohort (e.g. fixture 8, first purchase 2025-01-29).
3. `LEFT JOIN` the cohort to its purchase days in `(day0, day0 + 7]` and aggregate with
   `BOOL_OR`:
   - `retained_day_7 = BOOL_OR(purchase_date = day0 + 7)`
   - `retained_week_1 = BOOL_OR(purchase_date BETWEEN day0 + 1 AND day0 + 7)`
4. `COALESCE(..., false)`: for customers with no matching row, `BOOL_OR` over the NULL-extended
   row returns NULL, not false.

Working entirely in **dates** makes the calendar definition automatic: `date + 7` is a date,
with no hours or minutes involved.

## Three definitions people confuse

| definition | rule | fixture 20 (day 3, day 8) | fixture 21 (02-01 23:50 → 02-08 00:10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact day 7 (calendar) | a purchase dated day0 + 7 | no | **yes** |
| within week 1 (calendar) | a purchase dated day0+1 … day0+7 | **yes** (day 3) | yes |
| 168-hour window | a purchase in `[first_ts + 7 days, first_ts + 8 days)` or within `(first_ts, first_ts + 168h]` | depends on clock times | **no**: only 144h20m elapsed |

The hour-based variant gives a different answer for customer 21, and for generated customers
whose day-8 purchase falls within 192 hours of the first one. Dashboards that mix definitions
disagree by a few percent, which is why the spec fixes the calendar definition. Ask about this in
an interview.

## Edge cases (fixtures)

- **19**: day 7 exact → true / true.
- **20**: day 3 and day 8 → week 1 true, day 7 false. Day 8 is outside both.
- **21**: calendar day 7 although fewer than 7 × 24h have passed → true.
- **22**: a PENDING transaction on 01-31 is not a purchase, so the first purchase is 02-01 and the
  customer is in the cohort and day-7 retained. A query that ignores status drops this customer.
- **53**: day 0 = 02-28, next purchase 03-01 (day 1, crossing the month) → week 1 true, day 7 false.
- **28**: no repeat in the week → listed with false/false. An inner join drops it.
- Same-day repeat purchases are day 0, not retention. Including day 0 in "week 1" makes nearly
  everyone retained.

## Plan and indexes

The cost is one aggregation for first purchase over the whole fact, then a small join. At scale,
keep a `customer_first_purchase` table (insert-only: the first purchase never changes, unless late
data arrives with an earlier date or a status flips from PENDING to COMPLETED). Use
`customer_daily_activity` for the day lookups, via an index on `(customer_id, activity_date)`.

## Production considerations

- Retention for a cohort is only final once day0 + 7 has passed **and** late data has settled.
  Publish cohort metrics with a maturity date.
- Status changes (PENDING → COMPLETED later) can move a customer's first purchase. Define whether
  the cohort is frozen at a snapshot.
- Report rates as `count(*) FILTER (WHERE retained_day_7)::numeric / count(*)` and guard against
  empty cohorts.

## SQL Server / Azure Synapse notes

- `DATEADD(day, 7, first_purchase_date)`. There is no `BOOL_OR`; use
  `MAX(CASE WHEN ... THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)` and a `bit` / `CAST(... AS bit)`.
- `DATEDIFF(day, a, b)` counts **calendar boundaries crossed**, which matches the calendar
  definition here. `DATEDIFF(hour, ...)` gives the 168-hour flavour.
