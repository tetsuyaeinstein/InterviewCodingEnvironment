# SQL-008 · Top revenue categories with ties: explanation

## Approach

1. Join transactions → lines → products → categories, filtered to store 5, `COMPLETED`, and
   November 2025 UTC (`[2025-11-01, 2025-12-01)`).
2. Aggregate to **category grain**: `gross = SUM(quantity * unit_price)`,
   `discount = SUM(discount_amount)`, `net = gross - discount`. The discount is a per-line amount:
   do not multiply it by the quantity.
3. Rank by net revenue and keep the top two **levels**.
4. `ORDER BY net_revenue DESC, category_name` for a deterministic order inside ties.

## Three ranking functions on the fixture data

| category | net | `ROW_NUMBER` | `RANK` | `DENSE_RANK` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coffee & Tea | 78.00 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Electronics Accessories | 78.00 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| Kitchenware | 66.00 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| Stationery | 66.00 | 4 | 3 | 2 |
| Snacks | 60.00 | 5 | 5 | 3 |

- `LIMIT 2` / `ROW_NUMBER() <= 2` keeps two **categories** and cuts the tie arbitrarily (and
  non-deterministically between runs).
- `RANK() <= 2` keeps only the first level here, because the 66.00 level starts at rank 3.
- `DENSE_RANK() <= 2` keeps the two top revenue **levels** with all tied members: 4 rows.

Which one is right is a business question. Ask it out loud; the contract here says "top two
revenue levels, never cut ties".

## (b) Ranks without window functions (`SQL-008_alt.sql`)

`dense rank = 1 + COUNT(DISTINCT revenue strictly greater than mine)` and
`rank = 1 + COUNT(rows strictly greater than mine)`. This is O(k²) over k categories, which is
harmless for 12 categories and terrible for millions of rows. It is worth knowing because it
proves you understand the definitions, and it works on engines or interview whiteboards without
window functions.

## Edge cases and fixtures (store 5, Nov 2025)

| fixture | trap |
|---|---|
| 6001 Coffee 4 × 19.50 = 78.00; 6101 Electronics 2 × 9 + 2 × 30 = 78.00 | tie for first |
| 6002 Kitchenware 3 × 24 − **6.00** discount = 66.00 | gross 72 vs net 66; ranking on gross puts Kitchenware alone in 2nd |
| 6102 Stationery: product 8 on **two lines** (10 + 12) × 3.00 = 66.00 | grouping by line or `DISTINCT` product loses a line |
| 6103 CANCELLED 50 × 6.00 = 300.00 Beverages | without the status filter Beverages is #1 |
| 6004 in December | outside the month |

## Complexity and indexes

- Aggregation first, ranking second. The window function only sorts the k category rows,
  not the lines. The cost is the scan of one store-month of lines: `(store_id, transaction_ts)` on
  transactions, then the `transaction_items` PK.
- Filter in an outer query: window functions are evaluated after `WHERE`/`GROUP BY`, so
  `WHERE DENSE_RANK() ...` at the same level is illegal. PostgreSQL 15+ can stop the window scan
  early for `rank <= N` filters (a "run condition").

## Production considerations

- Category is a **current** attribute of the product. If products are re-categorised, decide
  whether history follows the current category or the category at sale time (an SCD2 product
  dimension).
- Revenue definitions (gross, net of discounts, net of refunds, ex-tax) should come from one
  governed metric definition, not be re-derived in every query.
- Deterministic output (explicit tie-breaker) matters for reproducible reports and tests.

## SQL Server / Azure Synapse notes

- `RANK`, `DENSE_RANK`, `ROW_NUMBER` behave identically. T-SQL has `TOP (2) WITH TIES ... ORDER BY net DESC`,
  which keeps ties at the *row* boundary (like `RANK() <= 2`), not two levels. PostgreSQL 13+
  has the same as `FETCH FIRST 2 ROWS WITH TIES`.
- Watch integer vs decimal arithmetic: `quantity * unit_price` with `decimal(10,2)` is exact in
  both engines. Avoid `float`.
