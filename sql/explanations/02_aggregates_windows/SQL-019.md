# SQL-019 · Product share of store revenue: explanation

## Approach

1. Revenue comes from **lines**: `quantity * unit_price - discount_amount`.
2. Filter to store 5, `COMPLETED`, November (half-open UTC range), then
   `GROUP BY product_id`. Product 8's two lines in transaction 6102 become one row.
3. A window total over those rows, `SUM(net_revenue) OVER ()`, gives the store total on every
   row without re-reading the tables. The percentage is `ROUND(100 * rev / total, 2)`.

| product | net | pct |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 78.00 | 22.41 |
| 2 | 66.00 (72 - 6 discount) | 18.97 |
| 8 | 66.00 (two lines) | 18.97 |
| 3 | 60.00 | 17.24 |
| 6 | 60.00 | 17.24 |
| 5 | 18.00 | 5.17 |
| total | 348.00 | 100.00 |

The rounded percentages happen to add to exactly 100.00 here. In general they may be off by a
few hundredths. Do not "fix" that silently; compute totals from unrounded values.

## Double counting traps

- **Header amount after a line join**: `SUM(t.gross_amount)` repeats basket 6101's 78.00 on both
  its lines (156.00 in total). Basket amounts cannot be split by product.
- **Joining refunds** (not needed here) multiplies lines by the number of refunds; aggregate
  refunds per transaction first (SQL-005).
- **`SUM(DISTINCT line_net)`** merges different lines with equal values: products 2 and 8 both
  earn 66.00, and 3 and 6 both 60.00.
- **Status filter missing**: the CANCELLED 6103 (50 x 6.00 = 300.00) makes product 4 the top
  seller (46 % of 648.00).
- **Percent of the wrong total**: a total computed after filtering to a subset of products.

## (b) Scalar total via CROSS JOIN (`SQL-019_alt.sql`)

Compute the store total once over the lines, then divide each grouped product by it. Same
result, two small aggregates instead of a window. The window version is shorter. (b) ports to
engines without window functions, and the same pattern gives "percent of grand total" for any
parameter set.

## Complexity and indexes

One range scan of store 5's November transactions via `(store_id, transaction_ts)`, a join to
lines by primary key, a hash aggregate. The window runs on a handful of rows.

## Production considerations

- Publish whether percentages are of net-of-discount revenue and whether refunds are
  included. Different definitions give different "shares".
- For dashboards, return `store_net_revenue` once and let the BI layer compute percentages, so
  rounding is consistent.

## SQL Server / Azure Synapse notes

- `SUM(x) OVER ()` is identical. `ROUND(x, 2)` keeps the input scale in SQL Server (cast to
  `decimal(9,2)` for display).
- Integer division: in T-SQL dividing two `int`s truncates. Amounts here are decimal, but cast
  explicitly when mixing with counts.
