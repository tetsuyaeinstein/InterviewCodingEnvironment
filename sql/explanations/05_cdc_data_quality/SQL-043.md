# SQL-043 · Late-arriving sales vs the report cut-off: explanation

## Approach

1. Read the cut-off: `daily_sales_report / ALL` = 2026-01-01 00:00 UTC.
2. Take completed sales with `transaction_ts` in `[2025-12-01, 2026-01-01)` (a sargable range on
   the timestamp, not a function on the column). Derive
   `business_date = (transaction_ts AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')::date` and `is_late = ingested_at >= cut-off`.
3. `GROUP BY business_date` with conditional aggregates:
   `COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE is_late)`, `SUM(...) FILTER (WHERE is_late)`, `SUM(...)` for the final
   total, then `HAVING COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE is_late) > 0`.

Fixture walk-through (customer 64):

| txn | business date | ingested_at | late? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6411 | 12-20 | 2025-12-30 | no: 10 days late vs its day, but inside the report |
| 6414 | 12-24 | 2026-01-02 | yes |
| 6412 | 12-28 | 2026-01-05 | yes (the only late sale that day: 80.00) |
| 6413 | 12-31 23:00 | **2026-01-01 00:00:00** | yes: the report used `< cut-off` |

Six dates are affected. 2025-12-31 is the worst (13.37 % of the day arrived after the cut-off),
which is typical: the last day before a cut-off always has the least time to arrive.

## Typical mistakes

- `ingested_at > cut-off` drops 6413. The boundary must mirror the report's own predicate
  (`< C` included, so `>= C` is late).
- Filtering late rows in `WHERE` makes `final_amount` equal to `late_amount` (100 %).
- Business date from `ingested_at` attributes late sales to January.
- Forgetting `transaction_status = 'COMPLETED'`.

## Plan and indexes

One range scan of December on `transactions(transaction_ts)` and a hash aggregate over ~31 groups.
The `FILTER` aggregates compute everything in a single pass. A self-join or two separate
aggregates would read the data twice.

## Production considerations

- This query is the **restatement trigger**: dates with late data must be recomputed (SQL-048)
  and consumers notified. Many teams publish "preliminary" vs "final" figures with a fixed
  settlement period (e.g. T+3).
- Track lateness as a metric: the distribution of `ingested_at - transaction_ts` per source. It
  tells you how long to wait before declaring a day final.
- Store the cut-off that produced each published report (report versioning) so you can always
  reproduce "what we said then".

## SQL Server / Azure Synapse notes

- No `FILTER` clause. Use `SUM(CASE WHEN is_late = 1 THEN gross_amount END)` and
  `COUNT(CASE WHEN ... THEN 1 END)`. T-SQL has no boolean type, so use a `bit` or a `CASE`
  expression.
- Business date: `CAST(transaction_ts AT TIME ZONE 'UTC' AS date)` on a `datetimeoffset`.
- `ROUND(x, 2)` behaves the same. Beware integer division if counts are involved
  (`100.0 * ...`).
