## Hint 1
Build the population first: one row per cohort customer with their 2025 completed spend, zero for non-buyers. Starting from `transactions` loses the zero spenders, and with them N changes.

## Hint 2
The equal-size definition is exactly what a built-in bucketing window function does, provided its ordering is total (spend, then id). The tie-safe definition needs, per customer, the share of the cohort spending at least as much as they do: there is a window function that returns precisely that fraction, and peers (equal values) share it.

## Hint 3
`NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY spend_2025 DESC, customer_id)` and `CEIL(4 * CUME_DIST() OVER (ORDER BY spend_2025 DESC))`. If you prefer integers, `COUNT(*) OVER (ORDER BY spend_2025 DESC)` with the default frame counts the rows with spend greater than or equal to the current one.
