# PY-006 · Daily totals by business date: explanation

## Approach (`PY-006.py`)

1. **Validate the zone once** (`ZoneInfo(business_tz)`), converting `ZoneInfoNotFoundError`, a
   `KeyError` subclass, into the contract's `ValueError`.
2. **Get an aware instant** for every row. Strings go through `datetime.fromisoformat`, with a
   `Z`/`z` suffix normalised for portability. Datetimes are used as they are. Anything whose
   `utcoffset()` is `None` is rejected. That includes date-only strings, which `fromisoformat`
   returns as naive midnight.
3. **Convert, then take the date:** `instant.astimezone(tz).date()`. `zoneinfo` looks up the
   offset *for that instant*, so the 23-hour and 25-hour days need no special code.
4. **Aggregate** count and exact `Decimal` sum per date, then emit in `sorted()` date order.

| UTC instant | NY offset at that instant | local wall time | business date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-03-10 03:59:59 | −4 (EDT) | 9 Mar 23:59:59 | 2025-03-09 |
| 2025-03-10 04:00:00 | −4 (EDT) | 10 Mar 00:00 | 2025-03-10 |
| 2025-11-02 05:30 | −4 (EDT) | 2 Nov 01:30 (first) | 2025-11-02 |
| 2025-11-02 06:30 | −5 (EST) | 2 Nov 01:30 (second) | 2025-11-02 |
| 2025-11-03 04:30 | −5 (EST) | 2 Nov 23:30 | 2025-11-02 |

Time O(n + d log d), space O(d).

## Wrong answers the tests are built to catch

- **`ts.date()` on the UTC value:** everything after 20:00 local in New York lands on the next
  day.
- **A fixed offset** (`ts - timedelta(hours=5)`): correct for half the year, wrong by an hour
  around midnight for the other half, and wrong on exactly the DST days finance cares about.
- **`astimezone()` with no argument:** converts to the *server's* zone. It passes on a laptop in
  New York and fails in a UTC container.
- **Treating naive values as UTC:** hides upstream bugs. Stored instants must carry their zone.
- **Returning keys in first-seen order:** the dict happens to look sorted when the input is
  sorted. The tests feed reverse-chronological input.

## Alternative approaches

- **Precompute day boundaries:** for a date range, compute each local midnight's UTC instant
  once (`datetime.combine(d, time(), tz).astimezone(utc)`), then bucket sorted instants with
  `bisect`. This is O(n log d) with no per-row tz conversion, and it is how you would partition a
  sorted Parquet file or build SQL range predicates. Beware: `combine(..., tzinfo=tz)` must handle
  zones where midnight itself does not exist on a DST day (historically e.g. `America/Sao_Paulo`).
- **In the database:** `GROUP BY (transaction_ts AT TIME ZONE 'America/New_York')::date` in
  PostgreSQL. Keep the predicate on the raw `timestamptz` column for range scans and index use.
- **pandas:** `ts.dt.tz_convert(zone).dt.date` followed by `groupby`. The tz conversion is
  vectorised.

## Production considerations

- **The business zone is master data.** A store's zone belongs to the store dimension and can
  change over time (SCD2). Multi-region reports group by `(region, local_date)`.
- **Late data:** a transaction arriving after the day is closed changes a published total.
  Decide between restating the day or booking it into the current period, and make the job
  idempotent by recomputing whole business days, not appending deltas.
- **Partitioning:** lakes are usually partitioned by UTC date. A local business day spans **two**
  UTC partitions, so read both neighbours.
- **Monitoring:** for each day, compare the count with the same weekday last week. DST-day
  totals look about 4% off by design (23 vs 24 hours), so annotate them rather than alerting.

## Follow-up questions

1. Each transaction carries a `store_id`, and stores live in different zones. How does the
   grouping key change, and where does the zone lookup happen at 100 million rows per day?
2. Finance wants every day in a range present, with zeros. How do you generate the calendar
   correctly across a DST change?
3. The lake is partitioned by UTC date. Which partitions must a job read to rebuild local
   business day D in `Pacific/Auckland`?
4. How would you push this computation into PostgreSQL while still using an index on
   `transaction_ts`?
