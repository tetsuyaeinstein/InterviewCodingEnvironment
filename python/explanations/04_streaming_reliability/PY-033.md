# PY-033 · Retry with capped exponential backoff: explanation

## Approach (`PY-033.py`)

A loop of `retries + 1` attempts. On a retryable failure that is not the last one:
compute `delay = min(max_delay, base * mult**n)`, apply jitter, check the time budget, then
`sleep(delay)`. On the last failure raise `RetryError(...) from exc`. Anything outside `retry_on`
is never caught, so it propagates with its original type and traceback.

Worked example: `retries=4, base=0.5, mult=2, cap=3`

| attempt | fails? | schedule | sleep |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| 2 | yes | 1.0 | 1.0 |
| 3 | yes | 2.0 | 2.0 |
| 4 | yes | min(3, 4.0) = 3.0 | 3.0 |
| 5 | yes | - | `RetryError(attempts=5)` |

- **Time:** O(retries). **Memory:** O(1).

## Why the details matter

- **Cap before jitter.** Capping afterwards lets jitter exceed `max_delay`; capping a jittered
  value skews the distribution.
- **Overflow.** `base * 2.0 ** 2000` raises `OverflowError`. Carry-forward capping avoids it.
- **Jitter modes.** Without jitter, all clients that failed together retry together (thundering
  herd). *Full jitter* `U(0, d)` spreads best and gives the lowest total load (AWS architecture
  blog); *equal jitter* keeps a minimum wait.
- **Non-retryable errors propagate unchanged.** Wrapping a `ValueError` (a bug, a 400) in a retry
  error hides it and, worse, burns the budget re-running a call that can never succeed.
- **`RetryError` with `__cause__`.** Callers need to distinguish "exhausted" from "failed once";
  `raise ... from exc` keeps the full chain for logs.
- **Budget vs retries.** A retry count alone gives an unbounded wait when each attempt blocks for
  30 s. A deadline caps the *total* latency, and must account for `fn`'s own duration; and it
  must not sleep when the sleep alone would blow the budget.
- **Injected clock, sleeper and rng.** Tests then assert exact sleep sequences. Never test
  backoff by measuring real time.

## Alternative (`PY-033_alt.py`)

Extract the schedule as a generator (`backoff_delays`), and let the retry loop pull one delay per
failure. Same behaviour; the schedule is reusable and testable in isolation. Libraries such as
tenacity model this as composable "wait" and "stop" strategies. A decorator or recursion works
too, but recursion hits the stack limit for large `retries` and decorators make injection of
`sleep` clumsier.

## Production notes

- Only retry **idempotent** operations (or ones guarded by an idempotency key, PY-032).
- Retry at **one layer** only: three nested layers with 3 retries each is 27 attempts.
  A retry *budget* (e.g. at most 10% extra requests) or a circuit breaker protects a struggling
  dependency.
- Honour `Retry-After` headers; log every retry with attempt number, delay and cause; emit
  metrics on exhausted retries.
- Distinguish error classes: 429/503/timeouts retry, 4xx (except 408/429) and schema errors do not.
- In asyncio use `await asyncio.sleep` and `asyncio.timeout`; cancellations must not be retried.
