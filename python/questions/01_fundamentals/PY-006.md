# PY-006 · Daily totals by business date in a local timezone

**Topic:** Python fundamentals · **Level:** medium · **Interview time:** ~20 min · **Module:** `python/practice/01_fundamentals/PY-006.py`

## Scenario

Transactions are stored as UTC instants, but each region's finance team closes its books on
**local business days**. A coin-counting transaction at 22:30 in New York on 14 June is a
14 June sale, even though it is already 15 June 02:30 in UTC. Regional dashboards are built from
a per-day summary, and on the two DST-change days a year the local day is **23 or 25 hours
long**. Those are exactly the days when a fixed-offset shortcut produces wrong totals.

## Function contract

```text
daily_totals_by_business_date(transactions: Iterable[Mapping[str, Any]],
                              business_tz: str) -> dict[date, DayTotal]
```

`DayTotal` is a `NamedTuple(transaction_count: int, gross_amount: Decimal)`, defined in the stub.
Any tuple with the same two values compares equal.

Each transaction has:

- `transaction_ts`: either an **aware** `datetime` (any tzinfo), or an ISO 8601 string with an
  explicit designator (`Z` or `±HH:MM`), e.g. `2025-06-15T02:30:00Z` or
  `2025-06-14T22:30:00-04:00`;
- `amount`: a `Decimal` (an `int` is also allowed).

Rules:

- The **business date** of a transaction is the calendar date of its instant **in
  `business_tz`** (an IANA name such as `America/New_York`). Local midnight belongs to the day
  that starts at it.
- For each business date, return the number of transactions and the exact sum of their amounts
  (no rounding).
- **Key order:** the returned dict's keys are in **ascending date order**, whatever the input
  order. Dates with no transactions are absent.
- **Errors:**

  | input | raises |
  |---|---|
  | a naive `datetime`, or a string without a designator (including a date-only string) | `ValueError`: never guess the zone of a stored instant |
  | an unparsable timestamp string | `ValueError` |
  | a `float` amount | `TypeError` |
  | an unknown `business_tz` | `ValueError` |

- Do not mutate the input.

## Example (business_tz = `America/New_York`)

| transaction_ts | amount | local time | business date |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2025-06-15T02:30:00Z` | 12.00 | 14 Jun 22:30 EDT | 2025-06-14 |
| `2025-06-15T04:00:00Z` | 3.50 | 15 Jun 00:00 EDT | 2025-06-15 |
| `2025-06-15T16:00:00+02:00` | 1.25 | 15 Jun 10:00 EDT | 2025-06-15 |

Result: `{date(2025, 6, 14): (1, Decimal("12.00")), date(2025, 6, 15): (2, Decimal("4.75"))}`.

## Corner cases to think about

- The 23-hour spring-forward day and the 25-hour fall-back day (transactions in the repeated
  hour).
- Zones east of UTC, where late-evening UTC is already *tomorrow* locally.
- Half-hour zones (`Asia/Kolkata`).
- Aware datetimes that are not in UTC.
- Input in arbitrary order.

## Expected complexity

O(n + d log d) for n transactions over d distinct dates.

## Clarify before you code

- Which time zone defines the business day: the store's, the company's, or the customer's?
- Should empty days appear with zero totals? (Not here.)
- Should money be rounded at this stage?
